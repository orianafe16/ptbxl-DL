\subsection*{Workflow}

Every interaction is recorded in \texttt{docs/AI\_USAGE\_LOG.md} of the repository (date, chat, who
was working, what was asked, what the assistant produced and how we checked it). For the theory and the
literature of this report the flow had four stages, each with its own role:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Perplexity} proposed candidate papers per topic (PTB-XL and its benchmark, architectures
        and training, learning strategies, autoregressive generation), without quotes or numbers. We
        downloaded the PDFs by hand.
  \item \textbf{NotebookLM}, restricted to those PDFs, answered one prompt per topic with, for each
        claim, a literal quote, the file and the printed page, or ``NOT FOUND''.
  \item \textbf{Claude} checked every quote verbatim, page by page, against the text of its PDF; only
        checked quotes enter the reports (\texttt{docs/literature/quotes.md}). A claim without a checked
        quote was reworded, stated as a hypothesis or left out; the closure of every such claim is
        documented in section~G of that file. Course materials are cited as course materials, never as
        the paper they mention.
  \item \textbf{Claude} also drafted the text of the reports from the block notes, the decisions log and
        the checked quotes; I checked every sentence against the figures, the tables and the logs, and
        every interpretation not backed by a number is labelled as a hypothesis.
\end{enumerate}
No output of one AI tool was accepted as verification of another.

\subsection*{Prompt strategy}

Each tool received prompts written for its role. Perplexity was asked only for candidate papers, ``no
quotes, no numbers, no invented identifiers''. NotebookLM was given one prompt per topic, always with the
same preamble, which forces a verifiable answer and makes ``not found'' an acceptable one:
\begin{quote}\small\itshape
Answer only from the uploaded sources. For each numbered claim below, return: (a) a literal quote copied
exactly, at most 40 words; (b) the source file name; (c) the page number printed on the PDF page (not the
viewer page). If a source does not contain the claim, write NOT FOUND. Do not paraphrase, summarise,
combine sources or add anything that is not in the text.
\end{quote}
All prompts are in \texttt{docs/literature/prompts.md}. The answers were then checked by the assistant
against the PDFs, which is the stage where the errors below appeared.

\subsection*{Errors detected (candidates for the AI Error Bounty)}

Each assistant error of the project is documented in exactly one of our four reports. The four sheets
below are the errors of the scientific assistants that concern my literature (dataset, benchmark, CNN and
generative models):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Two versions of the benchmark paper mixed} (NotebookLM; B12; Sheet P1).
  \item \textbf{A frequency band offered as support for a duration} (NotebookLM; B15; Sheet P2).
  \item \textbf{A secondary source quoted for top-$k$ sampling} (NotebookLM; B16; Sheet P3).
  \item \textbf{Statements attributed to documents that were not uploaded} (NotebookLM; B17; Sheet P4).
\end{enumerate}
The wrong page numbers, the article's licence quoted as the dataset's and the labelling rule stated from
memory by Claude are documented in Mar\'ia's PRDL report.

\subsection*{AI Error Bounty sheets}

\subsubsection*{Sheet P1. Two versions of the benchmark mixed (NotebookLM)}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.2em]
  \item[Prompt] For every model of the superclass task of Strodthoff et al., the macro AUC with its
        interval, the table and the page, with a literal quote.
  \item[Faulty response] The values of Table~I of the journal version attributed to the arXiv~v1 file,
        to its ``Table~II'' and to its page~4, plus a row (``naive'') that only exists in v1; one entry
        rendered as a symmetric 95\,\% interval.
  \item[Detected by] The assistant, when it compared the answer with the two PDF versions in the
        checking stage that we added to the flow (D8).
  \item[Proof] Table~II of arXiv~v1 (page~4) prints different intervals for several models; Table~I of
        the journal version (p.~1523) has no ``naive'' row; the paper defines its bracket notation on
        p.~1522 \cite{strodthoff2021deep}.
  \item[Correction] The published values were read from the rendered Table~I of the journal version
        (the table is an image in the PDF) and stored in \texttt{report/published.json} with file, page
        and quote; the bibliography cites the journal version, so pages match.
  \item[Reproduction] Compare Table~II of \texttt{2004.13701v1.pdf} (p.~4) with Table~I of the journal
        PDF (p.~1523).
  \item[Reflection] A number with a correct-looking source can still come from another version of the
        same paper; the version is part of the reference.
\end{description}

\subsubsection*{Sheet P2. A frequency band as evidence for a duration (NotebookLM)}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.2em]
  \item[Prompt] A literal quote supporting the duration of the QRS complex (the reason first given for
        a CNN kernel of seven samples).
  \item[Faulty response] A quote giving the frequency band of the QRS complex.
  \item[Detected by] The assistant, in the checking stage (D8): the claim and the quote were about
        different quantities.
  \item[Proof] A band in hertz is not a duration in milliseconds; no PDF of the folder states the
        duration.
  \item[Correction] The report states only what is ours: a kernel of seven samples spans 70\,ms at
        100\,Hz. The quote is used only for what it says, the frequency content of the waves
        (Section~\ref{sec:cnn}).
  \item[Reproduction] \texttt{s41598-025-26476-1.pdf}, p.~12.
  \item[Reflection] A quote can be literal and still not support the claim; the checking stage compares
        the claim with the quote, not only the quote with the PDF.
\end{description}

\subsubsection*{Sheet P3. A secondary source for top-$k$ sampling (NotebookLM)}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.2em]
  \item[Prompt] Prompt N4 (generative models), claim 2: a literal quote for top-$k$ sampling, with file
        and page.
  \item[Faulty response] Holtzman et al.'s description of top-$k$ sampling, given as the source, although
        the primary source, Fan et al.\ 2018, was among the uploaded PDFs.
  \item[Detected by] The assistant, in the checking stage of the literature flow that we added (decision
        D8); we re-read the corrected quote before using it.
  \item[Proof] \texttt{P18-1082.pdf}, p.~893: ``We randomly sample from the k = 10 most likely candidates
        from this distribution.''
  \item[Correction] Section~\ref{sec:generative} cites Fan et al.\ for top-$k$ and Holtzman et al.\ only for
        nucleus sampling (\texttt{quotes.md}, D3 and D4).
  \item[Reproduction] Search ``top-k'' in \texttt{P18-1082.pdf} and in \texttt{1904.09751v2.pdf}.
  \item[Reflection] A correct quote can still be attributed to the wrong paper; the primary source is part
        of the claim.
\end{description}

\subsubsection*{Sheet P4. Statements from documents that were not uploaded (NotebookLM)}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.2em]
  \item[Prompt] List the statements of the uploaded first assignment on learning strategies and whether
        the sources support them.
  \item[Faulty response] A list of ``statements'' that were the source definitions themselves, three of
        them marked ``NOT SUPPORTED'', although no first assignment had been uploaded.
  \item[Detected by] The assistant, in the checking stage (D8); no first assignment had been uploaded.
  \item[Proof] No first assignment among the PDFs of the notebook; the ``statements'' repeat word for
        word the quotes of the previous answer.
  \item[Correction] The answer was not used. The learning-strategies annex (Appendix~\ref{app:strategies})
        was built from the checked quotes.
  \item[Reproduction] Compare the answers to prompts N5 and N3 in \texttt{docs/literature/prompts.md}.
  \item[Reflection] A tool restricted to the user's documents can still invent the document it is asked
        about.
\end{description}

These errors were found in the checking stage that we added to the flow after the first round of answers:
the assistant compared each answer with the PDFs, and we re-read the corrected quotes before using them.

\subsection*{Reflection}

The checking stage changed which sources the report could use: several statements we wanted to make
(the clinical duration of the QRS, the first use of gradient clipping, the definitions of the GPT-3
paper) have no checked quote and were reworded or left out. The result is a report that says less, but
everything it attributes to a source can be traced to a page.
